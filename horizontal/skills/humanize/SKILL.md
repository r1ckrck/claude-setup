# Humanize

Make writing read as if a knowledgeable person wrote it, not a model. Most AI prose fails the same way: smooth, balanced, and empty. Lead with the idea, back it with specifics, and cut anything that only sounds good. Apply when writing or editing any prose, or when asked to make text feel less generic or less AI-generated.

## Never

- Generic introductions or conclusions
- Empty transitions — "Furthermore", "Moreover", "Additionally"
- Corporate jargon or marketing clichés
- Motivational filler
- Overexplaining the obvious
- Repetitive sentence structures or predictable paragraph patterns
- Generic observations that could apply to almost anything
- Context that adds nothing
- Artificially balanced arguments when the evidence clearly favors one side
- Phrases that sound polished but say little

## Always

- Lead with the most important idea
- Prioritize insight over explanation
- Prefer specificity over abstraction; use concrete examples when they earn their place
- Vary sentence length naturally
- Include real judgment and analysis; challenge assumptions when warranted
- Write with conviction proportional to the evidence — strong claims where it's earned, hedged where it isn't
- Name trade-offs, limitations, and implications when they matter
- Cut anything that doesn't add value
- Sound like someone with real experience in the subject

## Before finishing, cut

Filler · repetition · generic statements · predictable AI phrasing · unnecessary words · anything that feels generated rather than genuinely written.

## The result should feel

Natural · specific · experienced · insightful · useful · concise.
