# Design principles — spec

**Purpose.** Capture 4–7 real, reversible principles — each a statement, a rationale, and an
in-practice note — that let you say *no* to a design decision and explain why, in your own words.
A principle is a decision tool for when the right answer isn't obvious, not a slogan.

## Interview

Factor the brief's **intent**: a job-search portfolio may warrant a "sell it / market the work"
stance, so don't recommend principles like "let the work speak, even over selling" for a job hunt;
a personal showcase can lean more understated.

### [CHOICE] How many principles
- 4
- 5 — **(Recommended — memorable enough to internalize, enough to cover real tensions)**
- 6
- 7

Below 4 is thin; above 7 nobody remembers them.

### [OPEN] Tension harvest (the generator)
> "Name 4–6 design tensions you actually feel in your work — two good things that fight, e.g.
> 'speed vs polish,' 'range vs depth,' 'my taste vs the brand.' Principles come from real
> tensions, not aspirations."

### [OPEN] Draft each as an "even over" statement
> "For each tension, pick a side: '[X], even over [Y].' Both must be genuinely valuable — that's
> what makes it a real choice. e.g. 'Depth on a few projects, even over breadth across many.'"

### [OPEN] Rationale per principle
> "For each: why is this true for you specifically — not a universal best practice? What
> experience or belief earns it?"

### [OPEN] In-practice per principle
> "One concrete do / not-that for each — a decision someone could actually make differently
> because of it."

### [CHOICE] Statement style
- Imperative ("Do the hard work to make it simple")
- "Even over" ("Clarity, even over cleverness")
- Mixed — whichever fits each — **(Recommended — let form follow the principle; every one still passes the reversibility test)**

### [CHOICE] Scope of the principles
- About the portfolio object (how this site is designed)
- About how you work / design in general — **(Recommended — how you think is more differentiating; the site then demonstrates it)**
- Both, clearly separated

## Outline
1. Intro line — what these are for and when to invoke them.
2. The principles (4–7), each: statement · rationale · in practice.
3. How to use them — one line: tie-breakers for hard calls, not decoration.

## Acceptance test
Per principle:
- [ ] Reversible — a reasonable designer could hold the opposite.
- [ ] Forecloses an option — you can point to a real design it would make you reject.
- [ ] Rationale is yours, earned by context, not copied from a best-practices list.
- [ ] In-practice is concrete (an actionable do / not-that).
- [ ] Memorable — sayable from memory in one breath.

For the set:
- [ ] 4–7 total, no two saying the same thing.
- [ ] At least one would be genuinely uncomfortable to violate.
- [ ] None is a disguised feature list or a value everyone shares.
