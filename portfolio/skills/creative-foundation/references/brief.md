# Brief — spec

**Purpose.** Fix who the portfolio is for and what it must make them think, feel, and do — so
every later choice has a target instead of being made to taste. The brief is the contract a
stranger could use to reject an off-target idea.

## Interview

### [CHOICE] Primary audience
- A recruiter / sourcer
- A hiring manager or team lead — **(Recommended — the person who actually decides; "everyone" moves no one)**
- A prospective client
- A peer community

Always allow "something else." Push for the *narrowest real* audience; one primary, at most one
secondary.

### [CHOICE] What should they do right after?
- Reach out / start a conversation — **(Recommended — intent needs one explicit verb)**
- Advance you in a process
- Remember you for later

### [OPEN] Single-minded proposition
> "Finish this in one sentence: 'You should talk to them — they're the person who ______.' If it
> needs two sentences, it isn't distilled yet."

This is a **signature sentence** — offer several worded options and iterate until one is locked
(see `craft.md`).

### [CHOICE] Tone register (pick one primary, optionally one secondary)
- Restrained / understated
- Confident / bold — **(Recommended for senior positioning — assured without loud)**
- Warm / human
- Technical / precise

Then [OPEN]:
> "Describe the register as a comparison: '______, like ______ talking to ______.'"

### [CHOICE] Success criteria
- An outcome metric (e.g. interviews booked)
- A quality bar (the kind of role/work it attracts)
- Both — **(Recommended — one measurable outcome plus your own quality bar)**

Exact numbers may not be ready — they can be deferred (`> Deferred — set target numbers`).

### [OPEN] Scope — what this is NOT
> "Name three things this portfolio is deliberately not, and one audience or kind of work you're
> walking away from."

### [OPEN] Constraints
> "Hard constraints: must-include, can't-show (NDA), brand or accessibility requirements,
> platform, timeline, how much effort you'll sustain."

### [OPEN] Vision
> "In 3–5 sentences: the version of this portfolio that would make you proud — versus the
> weekend-default version you'd settle for."

## Outline
1. One-line summary of what the portfolio is.
2. Primary (and optional secondary) audience.
3. Intent — what they should think, feel, do.
4. Goals.
5. Success criteria.
6. Scope — in and out.
7. Constraints.
8. Vision.

## Brief signals

Three signals from the brief steer the recommendations in every later stage — capture them
explicitly:

- **Ambition** — restrained ↔ awards-level. Biases mood, decoration, motion, and the tech stack.
- **Intent** — job search / showcase / client acquisition. Biases voice and which principles fit.
- **Tone** — the register, carried into the voice.

## Acceptance test
- [ ] The audience is a nameable person, not "everyone."
- [ ] The intent contains a verb (a thing they do).
- [ ] The single-minded proposition is exactly one sentence.
- [ ] At least one success criterion is measurable and time-bound.
- [ ] The scope-out is specific (real things refused, not strawmen).
- [ ] Nothing in it would be equally true of any good portfolio.
