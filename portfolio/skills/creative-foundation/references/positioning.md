# Positioning — spec

**Purpose.** Fix the claim you can win on with this audience — a defensible "why you over the
alternatives," grounded in proof, not adjectives. Built on April Dunford's components mapped to a
person, plus an explicit anti-positioning.

## Interview

### [OPEN] Start from your fans
> "Your two or three happiest clients or teams — what did they have in common, and what did you
> give them that they couldn't get elsewhere?"

### [CHOICE] Competitive alternatives — what do they pick instead of you?
- A specialist (deeper in one thing)
- A generalist or a cheaper option
- An agency or a tool
- Nothing / status quo

**(Recommended — list all that are real, then rank the top one or two.)** You can't be different
without naming what you're different *from*.

### [OPEN] Unique attributes
> "What is true of you and NOT of that alternative? Favor combinations — 'designs and ships
> production code' beats either half alone."

### [OPEN] Value and proof
> "For each differentiator: 'which means [audience] gets ______, provable by ______.' Proof is a
> metric, an artifact, or a named outcome — never a feeling."

### [CHOICE] Frame of reference — which box should they judge you in?
- A familiar category you're the best fit for
- A hybrid of two categories — **(Recommended for a transition profile — the in-between is the point)**
- A niche you own outright

### [OPEN] Anti-positioning
> "Finish: 'People assume I'm a ______, but I'm a ______ — and the difference matters because
> ______.'"

### [OPEN] Trend (optional)
> "Is there a real shift making you more relevant now? Include only if it's true and the audience
> already believes it."

### [OPEN] The statement
> "One sentence: 'I help [audience] [result] by [how I differ], unlike [alternative].' Then the
> short paragraph that earns it."

## Outline
1. Positioning statement (one sentence).
2. Best-fit audience.
3. Competitive alternatives.
4. Differentiators.
5. Value & proof.
6. Frame of reference.
7. Anti-positioning.
8. Trend (optional).

## Acceptance test
- [ ] Names a specific audience AND a specific alternative.
- [ ] Every differentiator is false for at least one alternative.
- [ ] Each value claim has non-feeling proof.
- [ ] At least one differentiator is a combination, not a single trait.
- [ ] The frame puts your strengths at the center.
- [ ] Anti-positioning rejects a real box, not a strawman.
- [ ] Survives buzzword-stripping — a competitor couldn't copy-paste it.
