# Narrative — spec

**Purpose.** Fix the ordered story that carries the positioning — the throughline that turns
scattered roles into one arc. The narrative is the positioning proven in story form; it must agree
with the brief's single-minded proposition.

## Interview

The hook, throughline, and stakes are **signature sentences** — offer several worded options and
iterate until each is locked (see `craft.md`).

### [CHOICE] Who is the hero?
- You
- The user or team you served, with you as the guide — **(Recommended — "my journey" is the weakest frame; authority + empathy reads stronger)**
- The viewer themselves

### [OPEN] The throughline
> "The one thread true across every role you've held. Test it: does it hold for your very first
> project and your most recent one?"

### [CHOICE] Framing the transition (if there is one)
- Reinvention — "I changed careers"
- Continuity — "one thread was always there" — **(Recommended)**
- Convergence — "two paths met" — **(Recommended)**

Reinvention discards what makes you unusual; continuity and convergence keep it.

### [CHOICE] Ordering principle
- Outcomes-first — **(Recommended — lead with impact, then process; reframes you from executor to problem-solver)**
- Chronological
- Thematic

### [OPEN] Stakes
> "Finish: 'Done wrong, ______ happens — that's the cost I exist to prevent.'"

### [OPEN] The hook
> "Your opening line: the throughline plus one concrete outcome, landed in under five seconds.
> Pattern: '[outcome] — here's the thread that produced it.'"

### [OPEN] The arc in a paragraph
> "Tell the whole arc to a smart friend in one paragraph: where it started, the thread, the turn,
> and where it points now."

## Outline
1. Hook (one line).
2. Throughline statement.
3. Arc — origin, turn, now.
4. Stakes.
5. Proof beats (ordered outcomes-first).
6. Vision — where it points.
7. Ordering map + who is hero vs guide.

## Acceptance test
- [ ] The throughline holds for the earliest AND the latest work.
- [ ] The transition is framed as continuity/convergence, not apology.
- [ ] The hook delivers outcome + thread in roughly one sentence.
- [ ] Proof beats lead with impact, not process.
- [ ] At least one section makes the user the hero.
- [ ] Reads as a story only this person could tell.
- [ ] Agrees with the brief's proposition and the positioning's claim.
