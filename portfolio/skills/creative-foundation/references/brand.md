# Brand — spec

**Purpose.** Fix who the portfolio is as a designed object — its attributes, personality, and art
direction — so every later visual and written decision can be checked against one held intent.
Voice lives in the content-plan, not here; the site's organizing concept lives in `concept.md`.

## Interview

### [OPEN] Attribute harvest
> "List 5–8 adjectives you'd want a stranger to use after 30 seconds on your portfolio. Don't
> filter yet."

### [OPEN] Anti-attributes
> "Now the inverse: 3–4 adjectives that would mean you failed — words you'd hate to hear. These
> set the edges your real attributes push away from."

### [CHOICE] How many attributes to commit
- 3
- 4 — **(Recommended — enough range to be distinct, few enough to hold every decision against)**
- 5

### [OPEN] From → to (run once per chosen attribute)
> "For '[attribute]', complete: 'Not just [generic version] — specifically [the sharp edge I
> mean].' e.g. 'Not just minimal — minimal as in severe and confident, not empty and safe.' An
> attribute is worthless until it names which version you mean and which you reject."

### [CHOICE] Personality archetype (closest family, then refine in prose)
- The Creator / Maker — craft, building, making things real — **(Recommended for "craft survives into production")**
- The Sage / Expert — mastery, clarity, knowing how things work
- The Magician / Visionary — making the complex feel effortless
- The Explorer / Maverick — independence, doing it differently

Archetype is a starting compass, not a label to leave raw — translate it into how the brand
actually behaves.

### [OPEN] Personality in one sentence
> "Finish: 'If this portfolio were a person, they'd be the kind who ______ but never ______.'"

### [CHOICE] Art-direction mood
Read the brief's ambition first, then recommend.
- Warm / human (approachable, tactile)
- Cool / precise (engineered, calm)
- Neutral / editorial (gallery-like, content-forward)
- Bold / expressive (visually loud, signature-driven)

**Recommend by ambition:** a restrained brief → neutral/editorial (lets the work be loudest); an
awards-level / expressive brief → bold/expressive (the site itself impresses). Don't default to one
regardless of the brief.

### [OPEN] Aesthetic adjectives
> "3–5 adjectives for how the surface should feel — texture, weight, density. (Brand attributes
> are who you are; these are how the pixels behave, e.g. 'spare, high-contrast, deliberate.')"

### [OPEN] Reference pull
> "5–10 specific references you admire — sites, products, type, objects. For each, one line on
> what specifically you're stealing. We'll cut to a cohesive few; the edit matters more than the
> collection."

### [OPEN] What to avoid (anti-moodboard)
> "Visual moves you refuse — trends, clichés, looks you find dishonest for your work."

### [CHOICE] Decoration stance
- Zero decoration — every element earns its place functionally
- Sparing signature moments — one or two deliberate flourishes, otherwise restraint
- Expressive throughout — decoration as brand character

**Recommend by ambition:** a restrained brief → sparing signature moments; an expressive /
awards-level brief → expressive throughout, governed by craft (every move intentional).

## Outline
1. One-line brand statement.
2. Attributes — each as word → from→to → what it rules out.
3. Personality & archetype — family + one-sentence personality + how it behaves.
4. Art direction — mood, aesthetic adjectives, reference set with "what I'm stealing," decoration stance.
5. What to avoid — anti-attributes + visual refusals.

## Acceptance test
- [ ] Each attribute has a from→to — no bare adjectives.
- [ ] Each attribute is reversible (a different designer could want the opposite).
- [ ] The anti-list is non-empty and specific.
- [ ] The archetype is named and then translated into behavior.
- [ ] References each carry a "what I'm stealing" note; the set feels like one direction.
- [ ] No voice/tone content leaked in (that's the content-plan).
- [ ] A stranger could predict a design decision you'd make.
